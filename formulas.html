Algebra

Slope: m=(y₂−y₁)/(x₂−x₁) Point-slope: y−y₁=m(x−x₁) Quadratic formula: x=(−b±√(b²−4ac))/(2a) Vertex x=−b/(2a) Exponent: aᵐaⁿ=aᵐ⁺ⁿ ; (aᵐ)ⁿ=aᵐⁿ ; a⁻ⁿ=1/aⁿ Log: log(mn)=log m+log n ; log(m/n)=log m−log n ; log(mᵏ)=k log m Change of base: log_b a = ln a / ln b

Geometry & measurement

Circle: C=2πr ; A=πr² Arc length: (θ/360)·2πr Sector: (θ/360)·πr² Distance: √((x₂−x₁)²+(y₂−y₁)²) Midpoint: ((x₁+x₂)/2,(y₁+y₂)/2) Prism/cylinder V=Bh ; pyramid/cone V=(1/3)Bh Sphere: SA=4πr² ; V=(4/3)πr³ Similarity: lengths k → area k² → volume k³

sin=opp/hyp ; cos=adj/hyp ; tan=opp/adj sin²θ+cos²θ=1 Law of sines: a/sin A = b/sin B = c/sin C Law of cosines: c²=a²+b²−2ab cos C y=A sin(B(x−C))+D ; period=2π/|B| sin(2θ)=2sinθcosθ ; cos(2θ)=cos²θ−sin²θ

Literary terms, grammar, and rhetoric

Drill until you can name the device, the error, or the appeal in under ten seconds.

Figures & sound

Simile: like/as comparison Metaphor: implied comparison Personification: human traits on nonhuman Metonymy: associated name (the Crown) Synecdoche: part for whole (all hands) Apostrophe: direct address to absent/abstract Hyperbole / understatement / paradox / irony Alliteration: repeated initial consonants Assonance: repeated vowel sounds Allusion: reference to known text/event

Poetry & drama

Sonnet: 14 lines; Shakespearean abab cdcd efef gg Villanelle: 19 lines, two refrains Ballad: narrative, often quatrains Epic: long heroic narrative Iambic pentameter: 5 iambs (˘ /) Soliloquy: alone on stage, inner thought Dramatic irony: audience knows more than character Denouement: unraveling after climax In medias res: start in the middle

Grammar traps

It's = it is; its = possessive Who = subject; whom = object Comma splice: two independents joined by comma only Fix splice with period, semicolon, or FANBOYS Dangling modifier: opening phrase must match subject Agreement: verb follows true subject, not nearest noun Parallelism: same grammatical form in a series That vs which: restrictive vs nonrestrictive

Rhetoric & research

Ethos: credibility/character Pathos: emotion Logos: logic/evidence Claim → evidence → warrant Fact ≠ opinion; sufficient ≠ relevant Paraphrase: recast ideas + cite Quote: exact words + cite Plagiarism: uncited language OR uncited ideas Open-ended research question: not yes/no Primary source: original artifact; secondary: commentary

Writing modes

Narrative: scene, sequence, sensory detail Expository: thesis + explain; cause/effect, problem/solution Persuasive: position + reasons + counterargument Analytical: interpretive claim + quoted evidence Audience controls diction and tone Revise = ideas/organization; edit = sentences; proofread = mechanics

Periods (characteristics, not trivia)

Classical epic: muse, wanderer, in medias res (Homer) Medieval romance: quest, courtly test, marvels Renaissance tragedy: blank verse, aside, fall (Shakespeare) Enlightenment: reason, satire, social contract (Paine, Swift, Pope) Romanticism: nature, memory, solitary self (Wordsworth) Realism: money, class, probable social cause (Austen → Eliot/Twain) Naturalism: determinism, industry, chance Gothic: dread, threatened interiors (Poe) Transcendentalism: intuition, self-reliance (Emerson/Thoreau) Modernism: fragment, broken time, experiment Harlem Renaissance: Black modern art; vernacular + high form Oral tradition: portable moral; fable / myth / legend are not synonyms Trap: dates and spouses lose to “what artistic problem is this solving?”

Fallacies (name the move)

Ad hominem: attack the person, not the data Straw man: distort, then refute the distortion False dilemma: only two boxes when more exist Hasty generalization: n=1 treated as a law Post hoc: after this, therefore because of this Bandwagon: popularity as proof Slippery slope: small step → invented catastrophe False analogy: compared things do not match where it matters Circular: the claim restated as its own warrant Red herring: distract from the asked issue False authority: fame in one field used in another
