Where good students lose points

Exam trap guide

Acing is less about exotic tricks and more about never falling for the same distractor twice. Math and ELA each have their own cheap-point killers.

Back to Ace tracker

1. Algebra distractors

  • Extraneous roots after squaring radicals or clearing denominators — always substitute back.
  • Log domain — argument must be > 0; base > 0, ≠ 1.
  • Absolute value — |A|=B requires B≥0; two cases.
  • Inverse functions — swap x/y then solve; restrict quadratic domains.
  • Composition order — (f∘g) means g first.
  • Asymptotes vs holes — canceled factors = holes, not vertical asymptotes.

2. Geometry distractors

  • Scale ratios — lengths k, areas k², volumes k³. Mixing these is the #1 volume trap.
  • SSA is not a congruence theorem (ambiguous case).
  • AAA proves similarity, not congruence.
  • Unit conversion for area/volume — square or cube the linear factor.
  • Inscribed vs central angles — inscribed is half the central for same arc.

3. Trig & calculus distractors

  • Quadrant signs — ASTC; reference angle ≠ final answer.
  • Period — 2π/|B|, not |B|/2π.
  • Limits — 0/0 means factor, don’t declare undefined forever.
  • Derivative vs integral — read the verb: slope/rate vs area/accumulate.
  • Chain rule amnesia on √(g(x)), e^{g(x)}, ln(g(x)).
  • FTC sign — F(b)−F(a), not a−b.

4. Stats / probability distractors

  • Permutation vs combination — order matters? Use P : C.
  • Independent vs mutually exclusive — different ideas; exclusive ⇒ P(∩)=0.
  • Mean vs median with outliers — mean moves, median resists.
  • Observational ≠ causal unless random assignment (experiment).
  • Conditional — divide by P(B), not by P(A).

5. Testcraft (points free)

  • Estimate before calculating; eliminate impossible magnitudes.
  • If two choices are algebraic twins, one usually forgot a sign/domain check.
  • Flag and move at ~60–70 seconds average; return with fresh eyes.
  • Never leave blanks — educated guess after eliminating one option.
  • Last 10 minutes: only check marked items + unfinished.

6. Ace scoring mindset

Official passing is a scaled 220. Your practice target is higher: ≥85% raw on two full mocks per subject. That buffer absorbs form difficulty variance, fatigue, and a few bad reads.

If Mock A is 88% and Mock B is 79%, you are not ready — repair the weak domain that moved, then retake B.

7. ELA reading distractors

  • Topic vs theme — “war” is a topic; “war destroys innocence” is a theme.
  • Tone vs mood — tone is the author’s attitude; mood is the feeling created in the reader.
  • Main idea vs a vivid detail — a striking example is not the controlling idea.
  • Fact vs opinion in persuasion — “should,” “best,” and value words are opinions even when next to statistics.
  • Insufficient vs irrelevant evidence — a true fact can still fail to support the claim.

8. ELA literature distractors

  • Apostrophe vs personification — apostrophe addresses; personification attributes.
  • Metonymy vs synecdoche — association vs part-for-whole.
  • Speaker ≠ poet — the “I” of a poem is a persona unless the item says otherwise.
  • Fable vs myth vs legend — fable teaches a moral (often animals); myth explains origins/gods; legend has a historical kernel.
  • Period by characteristic, not date trivia — if it celebrates ordinary social detail, think Realism, not Romanticism.

9. ELA grammar and writing distractors

  • Agreeing with the nearest noun — “The stack of essays is…”
  • Comma splice “and” look-alikes — two independents need a period, semicolon, or FANBOYS.
  • Dangling opener — the implied subject of the opener must be the grammatical subject.
  • Revise vs edit vs proofread — ideas/organization, then sentences, then mechanics.
  • Yes/no research questions — NES wants open-ended, researchable questions.
  • Uncited paraphrase — changing words without citation is still plagiarism.

10. ELA oral/visual distractors

  • Hearing ≠ listening — barriers include bias, distraction, and rehearsing a reply.
  • Caption vs image — a caption can reframe what the photo actually shows.
  • Cropping and angle — selection is an argument, not a neutral window.
  • Copyright on student presentations — cite sources; “I found it online” is not fair use.
